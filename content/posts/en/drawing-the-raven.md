Ever since I started using Immersive Translate, reading English web pages has become much smoother. A translation is always one glance away, and language no longer seems to be in the way.

But after a while, I noticed that I was often just understanding things quickly and moving on just as quickly. I finished reading the information, yet the original sentences left little behind. Reading had become easy, but my English didn't seem to be improving along with it.

I wanted to change the way I use translation tools: try to understand first, and ask for help only when I get stuck; try to express myself first, then check where I went wrong. And the sentences worth remembering, I wanted to keep, so I could practise them later.

These needs don't stop at the browser, either. So I made Raven, a native macOS translation app. It's like a little raven perched on the menu bar, ready whenever I call it.

## Try to understand it first

I deliberately put myself in front of more English. When reading, I first try to work out what a sentence means from the words I know and the context around them. Sometimes I know most of the words but still can't make sense of them together, and I stop to think it over first.

When I really can't get it, I select the sentence, press `⌘1` to bring up the translation window, and then go back to the original with the translation beside it.

![The English original and its Chinese translation in Raven's translation window](/images/posts/68166834ab9e.png)

When I come across a sentence I like, I save it, and jot down where I found it.

For example, while watching *Clarkson's Farm*, I saved a line I really liked:

> Life's shit but I'm loving it.

When I save it, I add a note: "came across this while watching *Clarkson's Farm*". That way, when I read it again, there's a scene to remember it by. For me, a sentence saved together with its moment seems to stick better than one picked out on its own.

## Try saying it in English too

Besides reading, I try to write things in English as much as I can. My grammar and spelling aren't always right, but I want to get the meaning across first.

When I'm done and unsure, I select the text and press `⌘2` to check it.

In the example below, I deliberately wrote `I can` instead of `can I` in a question, spelled `improve` as `imporve`, and added an extra `make`. The check returns the corrected sentence and explains what was wrong with the word order and word choice.

![Raven checking an English sentence and showing the correction with an explanation](/images/posts/ea9b944e60de.png)

I look at what was changed and why before deciding whether to accept it. The AI sometimes misreads what I mean, too; when that happens, I can add more about what I wanted to say and have it check again.

Writing it once and then comparing it with the corrections is the only way I find out what I'm still not comfortable with.

## Practise the sentences you meet again

What I save while reading and writing slowly adds up into my own learning material.

I go back over these sentences every day. In Raven's learning mode, I can practise translating from Chinese to English, dictation, fixing mistakes, or putting words in order, and try to write out again what I've already read.

![A Chinese-to-English exercise in Raven's learning mode, with the saved scene](/images/posts/72f2e38716d6.png)

Something feels familiar when I understand it, but when it's my turn to write it, I don't always remember. Practising once makes it clear which words I remember and where I still slip up. Whatever I can't recall, I look at again and practise again.

I hope Raven can help me build this habit: think a little longer when reading English, try one more time when writing it, and when I meet a sentence I like, keep it and learn it slowly.

That's also why I made this little raven. It sits on the menu bar and helps me turn the bits of English I meet each day, little by little, into words I can understand and say myself.
